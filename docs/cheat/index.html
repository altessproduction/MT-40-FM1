MT-40X cheat sheetFM-1_23456

The Casio MT-40/41's auto-accompaniment on the M-VAVE FM-1 — firmware v1.8.2-mt40x, package mt40x_v1.8.2-0.4.fwsc, a fork of Felucca 1.8.2 with the 28-riddim bank of SLOOP Dub 1.6.

Quick start

The MENU does not open by itself: hold HOME (~1 s) to open it, the ALGORITHM knob steps between the tabs (DISPLAY · CONTROL · AUDIO · MIDI · SYSTEM), PRESETS moves between rows, KNOB 1–4 or OCT+/OCT− change the selected row's value. A short HOME press closes it.

  1. MENU > AUDIO > RIDDIM — one knob turn loads a riddim at once: the machine's three tracks (T2 skank chords, T3 bass, T4 drums — T1 stays yours, the MT-40 has no melody part), its tempo (66…172 BPM) and its MT-40 kind of sounds (Casiotone-style ANALOG tones on T2, T3; your LEVEL, PAN, FX sends and INSERT stay — tweak the track's OSC/FLT to taste).
  2. MENU > AUDIO > MT-40X = ON — the keys become the MT-40's bass keyboard.
  3. PLAY — or just press a key while stopped: synchro start, the first chord starts the transport.
  4. Press keys to re-root the whole pattern live. Nothing pressed: the riddim plays exactly as written.

The bass keyboard

KeyWhat it does
Any key (the 27 keys, MIDI 53…79 / F3…G5)Root of the whole accompaniment, major. The key itself sounds nothing — it re-voices the three tracks (128 steps). Its pitch class is folded into the bass register (MIDI 28…50).
The key a semitone above the held rootminor of that root
Two semitones above the held rootdominant 7th of that root (the bar's last fifth flattens — the turnaround)
The same key pressed againBack to major of that key (every root stays reachable)
Drums never transpose. Bass and skank chords follow the keyboard. MT-40X armed, the keys are no synth keys — play over it with MT-40X OFF, or from MIDI.

What a riddim loads

TrackContentSettings set on load
T1yours — the riddim has no melody (the MT-40's three parts only)nothing set (your sound)
T2the skank: the bar's chord (root, third, fifth, + seventh)GATE 40, VOICE POLY
T3the bass: the groove over the bar's root, re-struck as the machine plucks itGATE 127, VOICE MONO
T4the drums (one hit per step at most, original velocities)LEN 64, DIV 1/16

Patterns are 4 bars of 16 sixteenths (64 steps); the fill lives inside bar 4, as in SLOOP Dub (GLO+E4 FILL conditions stay available on steps you mark yourself). The bass never sustains: every strike is struck again right after, lighter — the machine's pluck (the Sleng Teng stutters on every sixteenth, that is its bounce). A remaining rest holds (TIE); a cut step ends the held note (the Sleng Teng's plucked tails).

SLENG TENG is the verified transcription (Noel Davey's own tab, the Ableton roll, 82 BPM): the root on the beat, three sixteenths a semitone above, the tail a fifth above, cut short. The MT-40's Rock preset is this rhythm — the riddim that started digital dancehall in 1985.

Known limits

  • The MT-40X state (loaded riddim, current chord) is not saved in the project — reload after power-off (the tracks themselves stay if you save the project).
  • The skank keeps the bank's register (57…68); chords follow the pressed root as a pitch class, not a register.
  • Patterns are 4 bars of 4/4; the MT-40's Waltz (3/4) is laid on that grid, as in SLOOP Dub.
  • The MAJ / DOM7 spelling of the grooves is derived mechanically — CALIBRATION.md is where you check it against a real MT-40.
